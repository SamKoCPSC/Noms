## About

- **Title:** About Noms — A better home for working recipes
- **Description:** Learn why Noms treats recipes as living knowledge: detailed, organized, versioned, and shared with intention.

### Introduction

**Eyebrow:** Why Noms exists

# A better home for the recipes you actually cook.

A good recipe is more than a list copied once and left alone. It gathers notes, substitutions, better timings, clearer instructions, and the small decisions that turn a promising dish into a dependable one.

Noms is built around that process.

### 1. Capture

## Keep the details that make it work.

The useful version of a recipe includes more than a title and ingredient list. Noms keeps instructions, timing, servings, equipment, tags, commentary, and photos together so the knowledge behind the dish stays with it.

### 2. Organize

## Build a library you can understand at a glance.

A flat pile works until it doesn’t. Collections and subcollections give recipes a place without forcing every cook into the same filing system.

Organize by season, cuisine, person, project, or whatever helps you find your way back.

### 3. Evolve

## Let recipes change without erasing where they came from.

Every published update becomes part of the recipe’s history. You can inspect an earlier snapshot, compare versions, and restore an older version as a new update.

The trusted version remains easy to find, while experiments still have somewhere to go.

### 4. Cook

## Adapt the recipe without rewriting it.

Scale a recipe for the people at the table or around the ingredient you have on hand. Noms adjusts quantities, servings, and timing for the cooking session while leaving the saved recipe intact.

### 5. Share

## Decide what leaves your kitchen.

Some recipes are private working notes. Some are meant for one link. Others deserve a place in the public collection.

Noms gives recipes and collections clear visibility choices, so sharing is deliberate rather than automatic.

### 6. Build on good ideas

## Give credit, then take the recipe somewhere new.

Public recipes can be forked into private drafts. The new recipe is yours to change, while its connection to the source remains visible.

That is the kind of community Noms is designed for: useful ideas moving between cooks without losing their history.

### Sign in {#sign-in}

## Sign in to open your recipe library

1. Select **Sign In** from the navigation.
2. Continue with Google or GitHub.
3. After authorization, Noms returns you to the page you originally requested or opens your dashboard.

**Expected result:** Your dashboard shows published recipes, private drafts, and your collection navigation.

### Create a recipe {#create}

## Capture the recipe while the details are fresh

1. Open **New Recipe**.
2. Give the recipe a title.
3. Add any description, commentary, preparation time, cooking time, and servings that are useful.
4. Add ingredients with amounts, units, names, and optional notes.
5. Add instructions in cooking order. Add equipment and tags where they make the recipe easier to use or find.
6. Save the work as a private draft, or continue to publication.

**Expected result:** A saved draft appears in the **Drafts** section of your dashboard. A published recipe appears in **My Recipes**.

### Work with images {#images}

## Add photos without leaving the editor

Add recipe images from the recipe editor. Images are processed and uploaded when you save. Individual steps can also carry photos, and image controls let you adjust the presentation before publication.

Wait for active image processing to finish before saving or publishing.

### Publish and choose visibility {#visibility}

## Choose who can see the recipe

- **Private:** only you can view it.
- **Unlisted:** anyone with the link can view it.
- **Public:** it can appear in Explore and on your public profile.

Select the intended visibility, then choose **Publish Recipe**. Draft edits remain private until you choose **Publish Changes**.

### Edit a recipe {#edit}

## Improve a published recipe safely

1. Open a recipe you own and select **Edit**.
2. Make the changes you need. Work is autosaved privately.
3. Select **Publish Changes** when the update is ready.
4. Use **Discard Draft** if you want to remove the unpublished revision.

**Expected result:** Publishing creates a new entry in the recipe’s version history.

### Compare and restore versions {#versions}

## Follow the recipe’s published history

On a recipe you own, open **Version history** to:

- view the differences introduced by a published version;
- open a read-only snapshot;
- compare selected versions;
- restore an earlier version as a new current version; or
- open the version graph for a wider view of the recipe’s lineage.

Restoring does not delete the versions published after the selected snapshot.

### Scale a recipe {#scale}

## Adjust quantities for this cooking session

Open the recipe scaler from a recipe page. Choose a multiplier, target serving count, or target ingredient amount. Noms recalculates ingredient quantities and applicable timing for the current view without changing the saved recipe.

### Organize with collections {#collections}

## Give related recipes a shared place

1. Open **Collections** and select **New Collection**.
2. Add a name, optional description, visibility, and optional cover image.
3. Create subcollections when another level helps.
4. From a recipe page, choose **Add to Collection** and select one or more destinations.
5. Reorder recipes or child collections from the collection page.

Non-private collections provide **Copy link** and supported-device **Share** actions. Deleting a collection removes its nested collections and memberships, but does not delete the recipes themselves.

### Explore public recipes {#explore}

## Browse what other cooks have shared

Open **Explore** to browse public recipes. Use the text field to filter the recipes currently loaded by title or description.

Creator links open public profiles containing the recipes and collections that person has chosen to share.

### Fork a recipe {#fork}

## Start from a good idea without overwriting it

Select **Fork** on a recipe page. If you are signed out, Noms asks you to sign in first. The fork opens as your private draft, ready to edit and publish independently.

The new recipe keeps attribution and lineage back to its source.

### Troubleshooting

#### My images are still processing

Wait for processing to finish before saving. If an image fails, retry it or remove it before publishing.

#### I cannot find an unpublished edit

Open your dashboard and look in **Drafts**. Edits remain private until you publish them.

#### Someone cannot open my recipe or collection

Check its visibility. Private items are visible only to their owner. Use Unlisted for link-based access or Public for discovery.

#### Search did not inspect my whole library

The current Explore field filters public recipes already loaded on the page. Full recipe, collection, and creator search is not part of the current experience.
